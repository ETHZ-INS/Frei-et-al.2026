
The lifelong maintenance of genome integrity is a particular challenge for post-mitotic neurons, making the brain especially susceptible to the consequences of DNA damage. Oxidative DNA lesions have been implicated in neurodegenerative and neuropsychiatric disorders, both of which are exacerbated by chronic stress. Yet whether oxidative DNA damage is temporally regulated in the brain and how chronic stress influences this regulation remains unknown. Here, we combined behavioral phenotyping, endocrine profiling, transcriptomics and genome-wide mapping of guanine oxidation to determine how chronic stress affects the temporal organization of oxidative DNA damage and repair in the mouse hippocampus. We find that oxidative DNA damage exhibits pronounced diurnal rhythmicity under physiological conditions. These oscillations are associated with transcriptional activity and DNA methylation landscapes and are accompanied by rhythmic expression of DNA repair pathways. Chronic stress dampens corticosterone rhythmicity, disrupts diurnal transcriptional programs and attenuates genome-wide guanine oxidation cycling. Although glucocorticoid receptor binding does not directly account for rhythmic oxidative DNA damage, our data support an indirect pathway linking glucocorticoid signaling to oxidative genome maintenance through circadian regulation of DNA repair. Together, these findings reveal the daytime dependency of oxidative DNA damage landscape in the hippocampus and demonstrate that chronic stress disrupts the coordination between circadian timing, hormone signaling and genome maintenance, uncovering a mechanistic link between stress and oxidative genome regulation in the brain.
